# Methodology statement, version 3

Methodology statement `methodology-3`, written 2026-10-08 for engine 1.77.0
and later (version 2 covers engines 1.72.0 to 1.76.0, version 1 engines
1.30.0 to 1.71.0). It says how the water-balance app turns a catchment's inputs into
the results its reports and evidence packs print. It summarises
`docs/model.md` (the full reference, by section number below) and does not
replace it. This file is never edited once published: a change is a new
version (`docs/methodology/README.md`).

## 1. What the model is

A **daily water balance** of one river catchment, modelled as a tree of
nodes (farms, stand-alone dams, other water users, gauges) that drain to one
outlet. Every day it works out:

1. the catchment's **natural flow** from rain, with a published rainfall–runoff
   model (§2);
2. each node's share of that flow, and what reaches it from upstream (§3);
3. what each node's water use takes from the river, its dam and transfers, and
   what it returns (§4);
4. the flow left in the river at every node and the outlet, compared with the
   **Environmental Water Requirement (EWR)** at each EWR site (§5).

The engine is deterministic: the same inputs and engine version give the same
results, to the bit. It rounds nothing inside the model (model.md §2.1);
reports round for display only. Flows are held in m³/day. Every run records
its engine version and a snapshot of every input, with a SHA-256 of each
input series, so a stored run can be re-run and checked.

## 2. Rain to natural flow

- **Runoff model: GR4J** (Perrin, Michel & Andréassian 2003), a four-parameter
  daily rainfall–runoff model, written from the paper's equations
  (model.md §2.4a). The earlier recession routine of the b023 workbook was
  removed in engine 1.0.0 because it did not conserve water at the event scale
  (engine-audit.md H1).
- **Rain used** is the catchment's own rain record. Days it lacks are filled,
  in order, from rain-source periods (another gauge for part of the record,
  §2.4e), CHIRPS satellite rain bias-corrected per calendar month against the
  catchment record (§2.4b), and the forecast (§2.4f). Runs of zeros that look
  like missing readings are treated as missing unless confirmed dry (§2.4c),
  and multi-day accumulations are spread over the days they cover (§2.4d). An
  areal rainfall correction may scale the gauge to the catchment (§2.4g).
  Every filled or changed day is marked in the run.
- **Evaporation**: A-pan evaporation (monthly, or a daily series, §2.3a),
  converted to potential evaporation with a pan coefficient, or a monthly PE
  row entered directly.
- **Land cover** (invasive plants, plantations) reduces the runoff of the
  nodes it covers (§2.5a).

## 3. Sharing the flow among the nodes

The catchment's natural flow is split into each node's own runoff by area,
by a high/low rainfall split, or by entered shares (model.md §2.5). Shares that
add up to more than the catchment refuse the run. Flow then passes down the
tree in calculation order, upstream first; a gauge only passes flow on.

## 4. Water use

- **Irrigation demand** is crop area × A-pan evaporation × monthly crop factor,
  less effective rain, per day (model.md §2.3). Each crop is on an irrigation
  system from the project's table (SABI 2021 systems, whose efficiencies the
  hydrologist may change, and rows of the project's own), by default or per
  unit; what is abstracted is each crop's requirement divided by its system's
  application efficiency, and a unit runs on its crops' efficiencies combined.
  A share of the water supplied, at most its losses, returns to the river the
  same day; the rest of the losses leave the catchment (§2.3, §2.7, audit N1).
- **Dams** store runoff and upstream inflow as the farm's settings direct, lose
  evaporation and seepage and gain rain on their surface (§2.7a), and follow
  their release and operating rules. A dam can be surveyed (a stage–storage
  curve) or estimated from its capacity.
- **Transfers and river off-takes** move water between nodes by structured
  rules (months, maximum rate, minimum dam level) the same day (§2.6, §2.6a).
- **Other water users** (towns, industry) take by seniority and position
  (§2.7c); **demand objects** add non-crop demand at a node (§2.7f).
- **Groundwater**: boreholes and their stream depletion, off by default (§2.7d).
- **Supply rules** decide the order a farm draws from its sources (§2.7e).
- **Development over time**: a dam's capacity may change during the run
  (sediment lost from its surveyed capacity, a dam that holds water only from
  the day it enters service), and a unit's abstraction may start on a date,
  so the record is run with the works as they were on each day (§2.7g).
- **Registered volumes** may cap a unit's use, or scale its demand to the
  registered volume in a full-allocation run (§2.12a).

## 5. The river and the Reserve

- **The daily EWR at the outlet** is a daily requirement, fragmented to the
  farms by their share of the natural flow (model.md §2.5). It is the
  pragmatic EWR (one flow per month) unless the project takes it from a
  Desktop Reserve Model output instead (§2.9f): the TAB file's monthly total
  maintenance flow, or the percentile tables read each day at the day's
  natural flow at the outlet, either scaled to the modelled catchment by the
  ratio of natural MARs or of areas. A run names the source and the scale
  factor it used. The days it is not met, and the shortfall volume, are
  counted at the outlet and each site.
- **Reserve compliance** at an EWR site with a rule table (a Desktop Reserve
  Model or gazetted table: maintenance and drought low flows, freshets and
  floods) is judged month by month by the Reserve's assurance rules: the
  requirement is read at the natural flow's percentile, and the month is met
  or not on volume and days (§2.9c, §2.9d).
- **Who is charged for a shortfall**: each day's shortfall at a site is split
  among the users upstream of it in proportion to their net impact; the part
  natural flow could not have met is nobody's (§2.7b). Curtailment targets
  are worked out from those charges over the reporting window (§2.11).
- The model **reports** the EWR; it does not protect it. Water users take first
  and the EWR gets what is left, as in the b023 workbook, so every failure is
  visible rather than hidden by a rule.

## 6. Calibration, checks and uncertainty

- **Calibration** fits GR4J's four parameters to an observed flow record at the
  outlet (gauge or logger), scoring the simulated outflow, with the farms and
  dams in place (model.md §2.10, §2.10b). The objective (by default KGE′), the
  window, the periods excluded with their reasons, and each day's quality flag
  (suspect, infilled, above the gauged range; §2.10h) are recorded with the
  fit. Split-sample validation is run with every automatic fit, and a dry →
  wet test when the record has at least 4 water years of 180 or more observed
  days.
- **Statistics** reported: NSE, KGE′, NSE of log flows and percent bias, with
  Moriasi et al. (2007) ratings and their caveat that the thresholds were set
  for monthly flows (§2.10f).
- **WR2012**: the natural mean annual runoff is compared with the scaled
  WR2012 quaternary reference, and flagged when it departs (§2.10c).
- **Data checks** on the inputs: agreement of the observed records, double-mass
  breaks, CHIRPS against the catchment rain by water year, zero runs and
  accumulations (§2.10a), and the hydrologist's plausibility checks (§2.10d).
- **Self-checks**: every run re-checks its own mass balance, each node's
  workings and the EWR charges, and records the result (the engine's
  `verify` checks). An invariant suite and a random-catchment soak test the
  engine itself.
- **Uncertainty**: a behavioural ensemble of GR4J parameter sets (GLUE-style:
  sets accepted by stated thresholds, with the seed drawn by the database and
  every start kept), whose bands are the range across the accepted sets. A
  change between two runs is the **paired** difference over the same sets
  (§2.10e). A band is not a confidence interval.

## 7. What the model does not cover

- Dam safety (classification and approvals for a dam with a safety risk,
  form DW793), groundwater beyond the borehole depletion above, water
  quality, and the socio-economic factors of s27 of the National Water Act.
- Whether any water use is lawful. The model runs the use it is given.
- A licence decision, which belongs to the responsible authority.
- Travel time in the river and canals: a day's water reaches the outlet the
  same day.
- The open items of the engine audit, printed with every run as the **known
  limitations** (docs/engine-audit.md), and the known engine bugs of the run's
  version, printed as **errata** (docs/engine-errata.md).

## 8. Assumptions a user sets

These change results and are printed with every evidence report: the runoff
model's parameters and their source (a stored fit or entered), the
calibration record and window, the EWR tables and sites, the flow-share
method, the pan coefficient or PE row, the CHIRPS correction and rain
handling, the attribution rule, the reporting window, the assurance
thresholds, the allocation mode, the development dates of dams and
abstraction, and the operating rules of dams and transfers.
